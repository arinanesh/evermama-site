The story behind EverMama

The moment that stayed with me

I keep coming back to one tiny moment.

My daughter was a newborn. She was crying, completely normal newborn crying, but every cell in my body was screaming, go to her, only you can comfort her.

At the same time, I was dealing with runner's knee that needed real strengthening exercises, plus all the regular postpartum recovery. And then my husband said, pretty calmly, "She'll be okay for a few minutes. Go take care of your knee. She needs you healthy."

That sentence stayed with me.

Until then, I'd been thinking of my health and wellbeing as something separate from motherhood, almost like time I was stealing from my daughter. In that moment, I realized it was the opposite. Taking care of my body was part of taking care of her. Being able to carry her without pain, lift her as she got bigger, run after her at the playground, and keep up with her years later wasn't separate from being her mom. It was part of the job.

Making recovery fit into real motherhood

The problem was figuring out how to actually do it.

Most postpartum recovery and physical therapy advice seemed to assume I could find a nice uninterrupted 30-minute block for exercises, or that my baby would happily lie on a mat next to me while I worked out. Some babies do that. Mine didn't.

And when you have a baby who needs you constantly, even exercises you genuinely want and need to do can start feeling strangely impossible.

That was when I started thinking differently about what taking care of myself could look like. The exercises could be tiny enough to fit inside the day I already had, two minutes here, a few repetitions there, something while the baby was on the floor, something while I was waiting for coffee. And the strongest motivation came from imagining the future version of me I was working toward: strong, capable, able to keep up with my daughter as she grew. That picture helped me recover quickly and get back in shape, which turned out to matter almost immediately. As soon as she learned to roll over at three months, keeping up with her became a full-time physical activity.

From postpartum recovery to longevity

Later, while I was pregnant with my second baby, I took a Stanford course on longevity.

The course wasn't about motherhood at all, but when it was time to choose a project, I knew exactly where my brain wanted to go: what would longevity look like if it were designed around the actual life of a mother?

The first version was this massive, complicated AI system that tried to engineer health into the cracks of a completely unpredictable schedule. It was clever and totally unrealistic.

I stripped it down again and again until what was left was almost embarrassingly simple: help mothers build tiny habits that fit into the lives they already have.

And that was when the idea expanded beyond the problem I had started with. Taking care of yourself as a mother includes strength, physical recovery, sleep, nutrition, mindfulness, stress, mobility, and social connection. All these ordinary parts of health matter for the long term, and all of them become harder to protect when you're taking care of a small child. If tiny habits could make physical therapy fit into a chaotic day, the same approach could make the rest of wellbeing feel possible too.

Once the habits covered all of that, another piece mattered just as much: timing. If 20 unexpected minutes appear, should I use them for movement, focused work, rest, or mindfulness? Your body has its own rhythms across the day, and a habit lands differently depending on where in them it falls. Lining tiny habits up with those rhythms gives each small window a bit more impact.

The idea becomes EverMama

That's where EverMama came from.

Not because I set out thinking, I want to build a wellness app, but because I wanted something I wished I'd had.

What stayed from that first experience, and became central to EverMama, was the idea of connecting small actions to the future they are building. A tiny habit feels very different when you can see who it is helping you become years from now.

Your future self, one tiny habit at a time

That is what I want to explore here: realistic ways to care for your health through motherhood, and how those small choices can quietly shape your strength, energy, resilience, and capacity over the long term.

I'm coming to this as a software engineer, a wellness enthusiast, a productivity coach, and a mom of two. This space is for anyone who wants to take care of her future self while living the very real, unpredictable life of motherhood.

If that sounds like you, I'm glad you're here.
